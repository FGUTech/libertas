# Section 702 FISA Mass Surveillance Authority Expires After Congressional Deadlock

**TL;DR:** Section 702 of FISA, which allows warrantless collection of Americans' communications, has expired after Congress failed to reauthorize it due to concerns over Trump's DNI nominee Bill Pulte.

## Key Points

- Section 702 FISA authority expired midnight June 12, 2026 after months of temporary extensions
- Program allows US intelligence agencies to collect foreigners' communications abroad without warrant
- Routinely sweeps up Americans' emails, messages, and calls in the process
- Congressional deadlock caused by Trump's nomination of Bill Pulte as DNI to replace Tulsi Gabbard
- Senate Democrats refused to move forward citing Pulte's lack of intelligence/military experience
- House also refused to approve even short-term renewal of the program
- EFF has consistently demanded warrant requirement for backdoor searches of Americans' data
- Bipartisan appetite for reform stronger than ever according to EFF assessment

## Deep Dive

## Background and Context

Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act represents one of the most significant mass surveillance authorities in US law, ostensibly targeting foreign communications but systematically collecting Americans' digital communications without warrants. The EFF has opposed reauthorization without warrant requirements for decades, arguing that the FBI's backdoor searches of this data violate Fourth Amendment protections.

## Political Dynamics Behind Expiration

The expiration stems from an unusual confluence of civil liberties concerns and political opposition to personnel choices. Trump's nomination of Bill Pulte—who has experience in housing finance but no intelligence background—to replace DNI Tulsi Gabbard created enough Democratic opposition to block reauthorization. This represents a rare instance where personnel concerns aligned with long-standing privacy advocacy to halt a surveillance program.

## Technical and Legal Implications

With Section 702 expired, intelligence agencies lose a key legal framework for collecting communications data that flows through US internet infrastructure. However, agencies likely retain significant surveillance capabilities through other authorities and previously collected data. The expiration creates legal uncertainty around ongoing intelligence operations and may force agencies to rely more heavily on other surveillance tools or seek individual warrants.

## What This Means for Privacy and Freedom Tech

The expiration represents the first major rollback of post-9/11 surveillance authorities and validates decades of civil liberties advocacy. For privacy-focused builders, this creates an opportunity to highlight the importance of end-to-end encryption and decentralized communications infrastructure that remain protected regardless of surveillance authorities. The bipartisan opposition noted by EFF suggests growing appetite for meaningful surveillance reform rather than rubber-stamp reauthorizations.
