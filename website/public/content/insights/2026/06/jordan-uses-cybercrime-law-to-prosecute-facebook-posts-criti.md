# Jordan Uses Cybercrime Law to Prosecute Facebook Posts Criticizing Israel Relations

**TL;DR:** Jordanian activist Ayman Sandouka sentenced to 5 years for peaceful Facebook post criticizing King's Israel policy, highlighting systematic use of 2023 cybercrime law to suppress online dissent.

## Key Points

- Ayman Sandouka sentenced to 5 years by State Security Court for October 2023 Facebook post addressed to King Abdullah II about Israeli relations
- Charged under article 149 (undermining political regime) and article 15 of 2023 cybercrime law for 'fake news targeting national security'
- Cybercrime patrols flagged the post on December 18, 2023; military prosecutor ordered arrest three days later
- Court of Cassation ruled charge should be lesser offense of insulting king, but State Security Court reissued original ruling
- Article 15 carries minimum 3-month sentence and fines up to $28,200 for content deemed threatening national security
- Pattern of systematic prosecution of Palestine solidarity activists since October 2023 using cybercrime law
- Case demonstrates how vague cybercrime provisions enable surveillance and persecution of peaceful political expression
