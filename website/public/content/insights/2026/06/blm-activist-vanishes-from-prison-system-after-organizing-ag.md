# BLM Activist Vanishes from Prison System After Organizing Against Solitary Confinement

**TL;DR:** Incarcerated BLM protester Malik Muhammad disappeared from tracking systems before surfacing 3,000 miles away in South Carolina, allegedly in retaliation for organizing a class-action lawsuit against Oregon's solitary confinement practices.

## Key Points

- Malik Muhammad, serving longest federal sentence of any 2020 BLM protester, vanished from Oregon prison tracking systems in March
- Muhammad later surfaced at Kirkland Correctional Institute in South Carolina after weeks at 'confidential location'
- Transfer violated plea agreement guaranteeing service in Oregon near support network
- Muhammad had been organizing fellow inmates for class-action lawsuit against Oregon's solitary confinement abuses
- Attorney Lauren Regan cannot provide privileged representation across state lines, severely impeding legal defense
- South Carolina corrections initially denied having Muhammad in custody, creating 'living ghost' status
- Pattern demonstrates how prison system can be weaponized to isolate and punish political dissidents
- Case highlights broader issue of people routinely disappearing within U.S. carceral system
