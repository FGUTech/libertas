# EFF Challenges State Social Media Bans for Youth Based on 'Shockingly Shaky Science'

**TL;DR:** Electronic Frontier Foundation argues that multiple states rushing to ban youth social media access are relying on flawed research and 'pop psychology' rather than settled science, threatening constitutional rights.

## Key Points

- California, Massachusetts, and Minnesota legislatures advancing bills to ban youth social media access
- EFF argues these bans rely on statistically flawed studies and 'pop psychology' narratives
- Legislators framing social media as 'public health epidemic' without settled scientific consensus
- Independent researchers from UC Irvine and Brown University find evidence is 'mixed, blurry, and contradictory'
- Large-scale meta-analyses across dozens of countries show no consistent association between social media rollout and declining well-being
- Studies fail to account for alternative explanations like pandemic isolation, school violence, economic stress
- Legislative push heavily relies on Jonathan Haidt's 'The Anxious Generation' despite his non-specialist background
- EFF emphasizes youth enjoy same free speech and privacy rights as adults under Constitution
