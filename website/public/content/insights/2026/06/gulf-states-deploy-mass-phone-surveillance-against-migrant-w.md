# Gulf States Deploy Mass Phone Surveillance Against Migrant Workers During Regional Conflict

**TL;DR:** Human Rights Watch documents systematic mobile phone surveillance in GCC countries, with random police checks, fines up to $3,200, and arrests for conflict-related content, silencing migrant worker communications.

## Key Points

- Police in Kuwait, Bahrain, UAE, and Qatar conduct random mobile phone checks of migrant workers
- Workers face fines up to $3,200 and imprisonment for photos/videos of conflict-related content
- Over 1,000 arrests documented across GCC states for sharing online content during regional conflict
- Employers warn workers to delete content and avoid social media posting about working conditions
- Surveillance extends to scrolling through phones during routine checks for banned content
- Climate of fear prevents workers from reporting labor abuses to human rights organizations
- Self-censorship now affects private communications, not just public posts
- Journalists report sources refusing to speak even anonymously due to surveillance fears
