# US Health Aid Tied to Surveillance Access and Pathogen Data Extraction

**TL;DR:** Human Rights Watch reveals US bilateral health agreements condition aid on broad surveillance access to health data and extractive rights to pathogen samples, raising major privacy and sovereignty concerns.

## Key Points

- US signed health agreements with 7 African countries requiring surveillance access to health systems data as aid condition
- Agreements allow unannounced inspections of health facilities and broad data collection without proper privacy safeguards
- Countries must share biological pathogen samples and data with no guarantee of equitable access to resulting treatments
- Aid can be withdrawn with 180 days notice if surveillance compliance fails, creating coercive leverage
- Multiple countries (Ghana, Zimbabwe) withdrew from negotiations citing data access concerns
- No prohibition on sharing private health data with US pharmaceutical companies without patient consent
- Agreements negotiated after US shuttered USAID in 2025, creating health crisis leverage
- Civil society organizations across Africa oppose agreements as 'dangerous' to human rights
