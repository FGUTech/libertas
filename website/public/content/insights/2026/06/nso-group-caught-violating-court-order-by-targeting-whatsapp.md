# NSO Group Caught Violating Court Order by Targeting WhatsApp Users

**TL;DR:** WhatsApp detected NSO Group conducting phishing attacks against its users despite existing court orders prohibiting such activities, highlighting continued state-level surveillance threats.

## Key Points

- WhatsApp identified NSO Group conducting phishing attacks targeting its users
- Attacks occurred in direct violation of existing court orders against NSO Group
- NSO Group is Israeli spyware company known for Pegasus surveillance tools
- WhatsApp previously sued NSO Group for exploiting its platform in 2019
- Violation demonstrates ongoing cat-and-mouse game between spyware vendors and platforms
- Court order violations could lead to additional legal consequences for NSO Group
- Highlights persistent threat to activists, journalists, and dissidents using WhatsApp

## Deep Dive

## Background Context

NSO Group has been under increasing legal and regulatory pressure globally, facing lawsuits from major tech companies and sanctions from governments. The company's Pegasus spyware has been used to target human rights activists, journalists, and political dissidents worldwide, leading to significant international scrutiny and legal challenges.

## Technical and Legal Implications

This violation of court orders demonstrates that legal remedies alone may be insufficient to stop determined state-level surveillance actors. The fact that WhatsApp was able to detect these attacks suggests improved defensive capabilities, but also indicates NSO Group continues developing new attack vectors despite legal constraints.

## Threat Model Considerations

For freedom tech builders and activists, this incident reinforces that even court-ordered protections cannot guarantee safety from sophisticated surveillance tools. The targeting of WhatsApp users specifically threatens secure communications for movements that rely on the platform's end-to-end encryption.

## Builder Implications

This pattern highlights the need for defense-in-depth approaches to secure communications, as relying solely on any single platform or legal protection may prove insufficient against well-resourced adversaries willing to violate court orders.
