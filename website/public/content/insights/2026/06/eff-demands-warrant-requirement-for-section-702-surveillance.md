# EFF Demands Warrant Requirement for Section 702 Surveillance or Full Expiration

**TL;DR:** EFF is taking a hardline stance on Section 702 renewal: either require warrants for FBI queries of Americans' communications or let the mass surveillance authority expire entirely.

## Key Points

- EFF demands warrant requirement before FBI can access Americans' communications collected under Section 702
- Current law allows FBI to query NSA-collected communications without warrants through 'backdoor' searches
- Congress has repeatedly delayed Section 702 expiration while negotiations remain at impasse
- Trump appointed Bill Pulte as DNI, who has history of weaponizing private government data against political opponents
- Democrats vow to block 702 renewal unless Pulte removed, EFF says they should demand warrant requirement
- If 702 expires, NSA can continue operations until March 2027 under existing court certifications
- Government would likely revert to Executive Order 12333 for overseas surveillance after expiration
- EFF argues letting 702 expire could create transparency opportunities and legal challenges to alternative authorities

## Deep Dive

## Strategic Shift in Civil Liberties Advocacy

EFF's ultimatum represents a significant tactical evolution in privacy advocacy. Rather than accepting incremental reforms, they're willing to let a major surveillance authority expire entirely if meaningful warrant protections aren't implemented. This hardline approach reflects growing frustration with decades of compromises that have failed to meaningfully constrain government surveillance powers.

## Technical and Legal Implications

Section 702 creates a massive collection apparatus where NSA gathers full conversations involving overseas targets, including communications with Americans. The FBI then queries this data without warrants under a 'finders keepers' rationale—arguing that since data was legally collected under one authority, they can access it without additional judicial oversight. This creates a constitutional backdoor that circumvents Fourth Amendment protections.

## The Pulte Factor and Political Weaponization

Bill Pulte's appointment as DNI adds urgency to reform demands. His track record of using FHFA data to accuse political opponents like Letitia James and Adam Schiff of mortgage fraud demonstrates how surveillance tools can be weaponized. This pattern illustrates why structural protections like warrant requirements matter more than relying on institutional norms or individual integrity.

## Post-Expiration Landscape

If Section 702 expires, the government would likely pivot to Executive Order 12333, a Reagan-era authority with even less oversight. However, EFF argues this transition could create legal vulnerability and transparency opportunities, potentially exposing how intelligence agencies adapt their collection methods when statutory authorities disappear.
