# UK's New Crime and Policing Act 2026 Introduces 'Cumulative Disruption' Protest Restrictions

**TL;DR:** UK's Crime and Policing Act 2026 allows police to restrict protests based on 'cumulative disruption' from unrelated previous protests, threatening sustained activism and civil society accountability mechanisms.

## Key Points

- Crime and Policing Act 2026 grants police power to restrict protests based on 'cumulative disruption' from other protests
- Law provides no definition of 'an area,' potentially allowing restrictions across large city sections
- Unrelated protests can trigger restrictions - climate protest could be blocked due to prior anti-war march
- Prime Minister Keir Starmer has signaled law may be used to block pro-Palestinian protests [Source]
- Human Rights Watch identifies this as latest in series of UK protest restrictions documented in 'Silencing the Streets' report
- Law threatens sustained protest movements that rely on cumulative impact to drive political change
- Provisions not yet in force - May 16 London demonstrations proceeded peacefully without these restrictions
- Creates framework for future governments to erode fundamental rights and democratic accountability
