# Meta Accuses NSO of Violating Court Injunction with New WhatsApp Attacks

**TL;DR:** Meta claims NSO Group violated a permanent injunction by conducting new spear phishing attacks on WhatsApp users, seeking contempt charges against the blacklisted spyware company.

## Key Points

- Meta accused NSO Group of violating court order barring WhatsApp targeting
- WhatsApp detected and disrupted spear phishing attempts linked to NSO
- Meta seeking contempt charges for violation of permanent injunction
- NSO Group develops Pegasus spyware targeting activists, journalists, officials
- US added NSO to Entity List in 2021 for malicious surveillance activities
- Attacks demonstrate continued threat despite legal restrictions
- Legal precedent could impact other spyware vendors' operations

## Deep Dive

## Legal Context

This violation comes after Meta successfully obtained a permanent injunction against NSO Group, representing one of the few legal victories against commercial spyware vendors. The contempt motion escalates the legal pressure on NSO and could result in additional penalties beyond the existing injunction.

## Technical and Threat Model Implications

The fact that NSO attempted new attacks despite the injunction reveals the persistent threat landscape facing secure communication platforms. WhatsApp's ability to detect and disrupt these spear phishing attempts demonstrates the ongoing cat-and-mouse game between messaging platforms and state-sponsored surveillance tools. The spear phishing vector suggests NSO may be adapting tactics to work around WhatsApp's end-to-end encryption.

## Significance for Freedom Tech

This case represents a critical test of whether legal mechanisms can effectively constrain commercial spyware vendors. NSO's alleged violation suggests that traditional legal remedies may be insufficient to stop surveillance operations targeting civil society. The outcome could influence how other platforms and governments approach spyware regulation and enforcement.
