# OONI Documents Women on Web Blocked in 7 Countries, Corporate Firewalls in 4 More

**TL;DR:** OONI research reveals systematic blocking of Women on Web abortion information sites across 7 countries, plus corporate firewall blocks in 4 others, documenting global censorship of reproductive health resources.

## Key Points

- OONI analyzed global data from November 2025 to April 2026 documenting Women on Web domain blocking
- 7 countries showing government-level blocks: Iran, Türkiye, Philippines, South Korea, Spain, Kuwait, Argentina
- 4 additional countries with corporate firewall blocks: Poland, Kenya, Pakistan, Georgia
- Women on Web provides self-managed abortion information and pill access in restrictive jurisdictions
- OONI updated Citizen Lab test lists to include reproductive health domains for ongoing monitoring
- Research documents censorship beyond website blocks including social media and search engine suppression
- Women on Web developed 'The A-Line' digital resilience strategy to counter censorship
- Study shows intersection of government censorship with reproductive rights restrictions
- Corporate-level filtering adds additional layer of access restrictions beyond state censorship
- Report demonstrates how technical measurement can expose systematic rights violations

## Deep Dive

## Context and Significance

This OONI research represents a significant documentation of how internet censorship intersects with reproductive rights globally. Women on Web provides critical information about self-managed abortion with pills, serving populations in countries where abortion access is restricted or criminalized. The systematic blocking across multiple jurisdictions demonstrates coordinated efforts to restrict access to reproductive health information, making this both a censorship resistance and human rights issue.

## Technical Implementation

The study utilized OONI's Web Connectivity test methodology to detect blocking across multiple networks in each country. OONI updated the Citizen Lab test lists to include reproductive health domains, creating ongoing monitoring infrastructure for this category of censorship. The research distinguishes between government-mandated ISP-level blocks and corporate firewall filtering, showing different layers of access restriction. The technical measurement approach provides concrete evidence of blocking mechanisms that would otherwise rely on anecdotal reports.

## Implications for Freedom Tech

The research highlights several key areas for freedom tech development: circumvention tools specifically designed for reproductive health access, mirror/backup systems for critical health information, and measurement infrastructure to track this category of censorship. Women on Web's development of "The A-Line" digital resilience strategy shows organizations are actively building technical countermeasures. The corporate firewall blocking in additional countries suggests traditional circumvention approaches may need to address multiple blocking layers.

## Strategic Considerations

This documentation creates a baseline for measuring the expansion of reproductive health censorship globally. The technical evidence provided by OONI's methodology could support legal challenges and policy advocacy. For builders, this represents a clear use case where freedom tech tools have direct impact on fundamental rights access, potentially driving user adoption and highlighting the real-world necessity of censorship resistance infrastructure.
