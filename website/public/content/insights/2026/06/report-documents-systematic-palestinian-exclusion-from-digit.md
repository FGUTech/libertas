# Report Documents Systematic Palestinian Exclusion From Digital Payment Systems and Infrastructure

**TL;DR:** 7amleh research reveals Palestinians face structural barriers to digital economy participation, including PayPal exclusion, restricted telecom infrastructure, and deliberate destruction of Gaza's networks.

## Key Points

- 7amleh published comprehensive report testing 30+ digital platforms for Palestinian accessibility
- PayPal operates freely for Israeli settlers but excludes Palestinians in West Bank and Gaza
- Palestinians restricted to 2G/3G infrastructure while Israelis access 4G/5G networks
- 81% of Gaza's telecommunications network destroyed by April 2026, per EU/UN assessment
- Palestinian ICT sector employed 9,000 people across 700 companies before October 2023
- Digital workers in Gaza resort to solar-powered coworking spaces and long walks for connectivity
- Platform exclusion forces reliance on costly intermediaries and informal payment systems
- Infrastructure control prevents Palestinian sovereignty over critical digital networks

## Deep Dive

## Systematic Digital Apartheid

The 7amleh report provides documented evidence of what researchers term "structural exclusion" from the digital economy. This goes beyond simple access issues—it represents deliberate policy choices that maintain economic control. The PayPal exclusion is particularly stark: the platform operates freely for Israeli citizens, including illegal West Bank settlers, while systematically denying service to Palestinians in the same geographic area [Source].

## Infrastructure as Control Mechanism

Palestinians lack sovereignty over their telecommunications infrastructure, creating cascading effects throughout the digital economy. While Israelis benefit from advanced 4G and 5G networks, Palestinians in the West Bank only recently gained limited 3G access, and Gaza remains largely restricted to 2G infrastructure [Source]. This disparity directly undermines remote work capabilities, client relationships, and e-commerce participation.

## Economic Impact and Resilience

The research documents how digital work became critical for Palestinian economic survival, with the ICT sector contributing 4% of Palestinian GDP and employing nearly 9,000 people across 700 companies before October 2023 [Source]. The destruction of 81% of Gaza's telecommunications infrastructure represents not just infrastructure damage but systematic economic warfare through digital means.

## Implications for Freedom Tech

This case study demonstrates how platform policies and infrastructure control function as tools of systematic oppression. The requirement for Palestinian digital workers to compete globally while being denied equal payment access, infrastructure, and connectivity reveals the political nature of seemingly neutral technology policies. For freedom tech builders, this highlights the critical importance of censorship-resistant payment systems and mesh networking solutions that don't rely on centralized infrastructure control.
