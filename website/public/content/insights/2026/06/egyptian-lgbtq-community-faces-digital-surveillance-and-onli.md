# Egyptian LGBTQ+ Community Faces Digital Surveillance and Online Harassment

**TL;DR:** LGBTQ+ individuals in Egypt navigate severe digital risks from state surveillance via cybercrime laws and coordinated anti-LGBTQ+ campaigns, forcing community isolation and adoption of encrypted communications.

## Key Points

- Egypt criminalizes same-sex relations under prostitution and cybercrime laws enforced through Economic Courts
- LGBTQ+ Egyptians face state surveillance, far-right targeting, and online harassment forcing digital isolation
- Organized 'Fetrah' campaign launched coordinated anti-LGBTQ+ attacks across Twitter, Instagram, Facebook, and Telegram
- Community members teach digital security practices including Signal/Wire encryption and operational security
- Cybercrime laws increasingly applied to target LGBTQ+ individuals online since 2019
- Many queer Egyptians delete social media accounts and rebuild identities for safety
- Threats come from multiple vectors: state actors, religious groups, and even within LGBTQ+ community
- Digital safety practices include avoiding personal information sharing, location masking, and two-factor authentication
