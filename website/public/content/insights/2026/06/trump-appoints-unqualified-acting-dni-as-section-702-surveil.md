# Trump Appoints Unqualified Acting DNI as Section 702 Surveillance Powers Face Renewal

**TL;DR:** Trump appointed Bill Pulte, who lacks intelligence experience but has weaponized government data against political foes, as acting DNI as Congress debates renewing Section 702 warrantless surveillance powers.

## Key Points

- Trump named Bill Pulte, current FHFA director, as acting Director of National Intelligence replacing Tulsi Gabbard
- Pulte lacks required intelligence, military, or congressional experience per 2004 DNI creation law
- As FHFA director, Pulte used private mortgage data to accuse Trump political opponents of fraud without evidence
- Accusations against Letitia James, Adam Schiff, and others led to failed prosecutions or no charges
- Congress faces June 12 deadline to reauthorize Section 702 warrantless surveillance program
- Section 702 allows NSA to collect Americans' communications and FBI to query without warrants
- Acting appointment bypasses Senate confirmation; Pulte could serve ~7 months under Vacancies Act
- EFF warns unqualified, politically motivated DNI poses threat to civil liberties with access to classified data

## Deep Dive

## Context and Threat Assessment

The timing of this appointment creates a perfect storm for surveillance abuse. As Congress debates reauthorizing Section 702 - a program that allows warrantless collection of Americans' communications when they contact foreign targets - Trump has installed someone with zero intelligence experience but a documented history of weaponizing government data for political purposes. Pulte's track record at FHFA shows a pattern of using private information to attack Trump's political enemies, including failed attempts to prosecute NY AG Letitia James and baseless fraud accusations against Sen. Adam Schiff and Fed governor Lisa Cook.

## Technical and Legal Implications

Section 702 represents one of the most invasive surveillance authorities in US law, allowing the NSA to collect communications and store them in databases accessible by other agencies including the FBI - all without warrants for US persons' data. The program's "backdoor search" loophole lets the FBI query these databases for Americans' communications without judicial oversight. An acting DNI with no intelligence oversight experience and demonstrated willingness to abuse government data access could dramatically expand these abuses.

## What This Means for Privacy and Civil Liberties

The combination of an unqualified, politically motivated acting DNI and potential Section 702 reauthorization creates unprecedented risk for Americans' privacy rights. Unlike previous DNIs with intelligence community experience who understood legal constraints, Pulte's background suggests he views government data as a political weapon rather than something requiring careful oversight. His ability to serve without Senate confirmation for up to seven months means he could oversee Section 702 operations with minimal accountability during a critical reauthorization period.
