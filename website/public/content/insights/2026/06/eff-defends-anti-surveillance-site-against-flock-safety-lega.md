# EFF Defends Anti-Surveillance Site Against Flock Safety Legal Threats

**TL;DR:** EFF successfully defended DeFlock.me, a grassroots website tracking ALPR surveillance cameras, against cease-and-desist threats from police tech company Flock Safety.

## Key Points

- Will Freeman created DeFlock.me to crowdsource locations of automated license plate reader (ALPR) cameras
- Flock Safety sent cease-and-desist letter citing trademark law to silence the tracking site
- EFF stepped in to provide legal defense, recognizing it as protected First Amendment criticism
- Flock Safety backed down after EFF's intervention ('Big Surveillance Tech flinched')
- DeFlock.me has expanded efforts to expose mass surveillance infrastructure since the legal victory
- Case demonstrates successful pattern of corporate intimidation followed by retreat when challenged
- Privacy advocates are winning battles against ALPR contracts in cities nationwide

## Deep Dive

## Context and Background

This case represents a textbook example of how surveillance companies attempt to silence critics through legal intimidation, and how organized legal defense can counter these tactics. DeFlock.me operates by crowdsourcing the locations of Flock Safety's ALPR cameras, creating transparency around surveillance infrastructure that operates largely in the shadows. The site's approach of 'turning the tables' on surveillance companies by making their own infrastructure visible represents an effective form of digital resistance.

## Legal Strategy and Implications

Flock Safety's use of trademark law to target criticism follows a common corporate playbook: deploy legal resources to intimidate smaller actors who lack the resources for prolonged legal battles. EFF's intervention demonstrates the critical importance of legal defense organizations in protecting grassroots digital rights activism. The company's retreat suggests these intimidation tactics are primarily effective against isolated individuals rather than organized resistance.

## Technical and Tactical Significance

The success of DeFlock.me validates crowdsourced surveillance mapping as both a technical approach and advocacy tool. By making surveillance infrastructure visible and searchable, the project shifts the information asymmetry that typically favors surveillance operators. The expansion of the project following the legal victory suggests that successful defense against corporate intimidation can strengthen rather than diminish resistance efforts.

## Broader Movement Context

This case occurs within a broader pushback against ALPR deployment, with privacy advocates successfully challenging contracts in multiple cities. The combination of legal defense, technical tools, and local advocacy represents a multi-layered approach to resisting surveillance infrastructure that other movements could replicate.
