# Indonesian Authorities Disrupt Documentary Screenings, Allegedly Coerce Featured Activist

**TL;DR:** Indonesian police and military systematically disrupted screenings of 'Pig Feast' documentary criticizing government Papua policies, while activist Yasinta Moiwend disappeared then filed complaint against filmmakers alongside intelligence officials.

## Key Points

- Activist Yasinta Moiwend went missing May 23 from her home in Merauke, South Papua
- She reappeared May 29 in Jakarta filing police complaint against 'Pig Feast' documentary featuring her
- Moiwend appeared alongside State Intelligence Agency and ruling Gerindra Party members
- Family believes she was coerced and manipulated into filing the complaint [Source]
- Indonesian military and police banned dozens of documentary screenings since April
- Documentary criticizes government's Papua land grab policies and deforestation projects
- Military chief cited 'security considerations' for disrupting screenings across multiple regions
- Pattern shows coordinated government response to suppress critical documentary content

## Deep Dive

## Government Information Control Operations

This case demonstrates sophisticated government tactics for suppressing critical media content that goes beyond simple censorship. Indonesian authorities deployed multiple vectors: physical disruption of screenings, alleged coercion of featured subjects to discredit the work, and coordination between intelligence agencies and political parties to create legal pressure on filmmakers.

## Surveillance and Coercion Indicators

The circumstances of Yasinta Moiwend's disappearance and reappearance raise serious red flags about state coercion. Her family's assessment that she was manipulated, combined with her appearance alongside State Intelligence Agency officials, suggests a coordinated effort to weaponize the activist against the documentary. This represents an evolution in suppression tactics where governments turn subjects against documentarians rather than simply banning content.

## Implications for Information Freedom

The systematic nature of the screening disruptions—spanning universities, journalist gatherings, and public venues across multiple islands—indicates centralized coordination. The military's open acknowledgment of 'security considerations' for disrupting film screenings normalizes censorship of critical documentary content. This case study reveals how governments can simultaneously attack content distribution (screening bans), production credibility (coerced complaints), and creator safety (surveillance of featured subjects).

## What This Means for Builders

This demonstrates why decentralized, censorship-resistant distribution methods are critical for documentary content in authoritarian contexts. The government's ability to systematically disrupt physical screenings and manipulate legal processes shows traditional distribution channels' vulnerability. The coordination between intelligence agencies, military, and ruling party officials illustrates the comprehensive nature of state information control capabilities.
