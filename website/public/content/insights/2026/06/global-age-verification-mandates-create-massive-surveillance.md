# Global Age Verification Mandates Create Massive Surveillance Infrastructure

**TL;DR:** Governments worldwide are implementing age verification systems requiring identity checks to access online platforms, creating unprecedented surveillance infrastructure that threatens privacy and anonymity for all users.

## Key Points

- Australia banned under-16s from social media with $32M USD fines for non-compliant platforms
- UK's Online Safety Act mandates age checks for any service with content deemed harmful to children
- Indonesia, Malaysia following with similar under-16 bans on major platforms
- Brazil's new law requires age verification for any service that could be accessed by minors
- EU developing interoperable age verification 'app' for continent-wide implementation
- Major platforms (Meta, Google, X, TikTok) complying by implementing identity verification systems
- Reddit challenging Australia's law on constitutional grounds in court
- Age verification technically requires collecting identity data from all users, not just minors

## Deep Dive

## Context and Scale

The global push for age verification represents one of the most significant threats to online anonymity and privacy in the internet's history. What politicians frame as child protection is functionally requiring identity verification for internet access—a fundamental shift from the internet's pseudonymous design principles. The EFF notes this creates "massive surveillance infrastructure" that affects all users, not just minors.

## Technical and Privacy Implications

Age verification systems cannot determine if someone is under a threshold age without collecting identity information from all users. Even "privacy-preserving" approaches like age estimation or cryptographic proofs require substantial data collection and create new attack vectors. The UK's struggle to find effective methods demonstrates the technical impossibility of age verification without surveillance. Brazil's law is particularly concerning as it covers any service "likely to be accessed" by minors, potentially encompassing most of the internet.

## Threat Model Analysis

These mandates create several critical threats: (1) Identity databases linking real names to online activity, (2) Barriers to accessing information and digital services, (3) Expansion of government control over internet access, and (4) Precedent for broader identity requirements. The compliance by major platforms creates infrastructure that can be repurposed for broader surveillance and control.

## Builder Considerations

The global nature of these mandates means freedom tech builders must consider age verification requirements in system design. Decentralized and privacy-preserving systems become more valuable as centralized platforms implement identity verification. However, even alternative systems may face pressure to comply when operating in these jurisdictions. The Brazil case is particularly concerning for open source projects, as the law's broad scope could affect free software development.
