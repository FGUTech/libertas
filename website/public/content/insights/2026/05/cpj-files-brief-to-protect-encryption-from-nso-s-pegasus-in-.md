# CPJ Files Brief to Protect Encryption from NSO's Pegasus in WhatsApp Case

**TL;DR:** Committee to Protect Journalists and 10 civil society orgs filed amicus brief supporting permanent injunction against NSO Group to protect WhatsApp encryption from Pegasus spyware targeting journalists.

## Key Points

- CPJ and 10 civil society organizations filed amicus brief in U.S. Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals
- Brief supports permanent injunction preventing NSO Group from targeting WhatsApp encryption
- NSO previously used WhatsApp servers to deliver Pegasus spyware to 1,400+ people across 20 countries
- Multiple journalists among confirmed targets of the surveillance campaign
- District Court ruled in favor of WhatsApp in 2025 after 6-year legal battle
- Permanent injunction issued stopping NSO from targeting WhatsApp and its users
- NSO's appeal to U.S. Supreme Court was unsuccessful
- Brief argues encryption protection essential for journalist safety and human rights defenders

## Deep Dive

## Legal Precedent for Encryption Protection

This case represents a significant legal victory for encryption technology, establishing precedent that commercial spyware companies cannot exploit messaging platforms to deliver surveillance tools. The permanent injunction against NSO Group creates enforceable boundaries around one of the world's most widely-used encrypted messaging platforms, with implications extending far beyond WhatsApp to the broader encrypted communications ecosystem.

## Technical and Strategic Implications

NSO's Pegasus deployment method—hijacking WhatsApp's own infrastructure to deliver zero-click exploits—demonstrated sophisticated supply chain attacks against encrypted platforms. The legal victory suggests courts are willing to protect the integrity of encryption systems when presented with clear evidence of abuse. However, the ruling's technical scope remains limited to NSO's specific methods against WhatsApp, potentially leaving room for other actors using different vectors.

## Broader Context for Freedom Tech

The six-year legal timeline and multiple court levels required to achieve this protection highlight both the challenges and possibilities in defending freedom tech through legal channels. CPJ's involvement brings documented evidence of real-world harm to journalists, strengthening the case for encryption as essential infrastructure rather than optional privacy enhancement. The coalition approach—11 organizations filing together—demonstrates coordinated civil society response to surveillance technology threats.

## What Builders Should Monitor

This precedent may influence how courts evaluate other cases involving commercial spyware and encrypted platforms. However, enforcement remains challenging given NSO's international operations and the broader ecosystem of surveillance vendors. The technical arms race continues, with this ruling addressing past NSO methods while new exploitation techniques continue developing.
