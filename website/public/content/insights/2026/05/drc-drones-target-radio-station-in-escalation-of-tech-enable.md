# DRC Drones Target Radio Station in Escalation of Tech-Enabled Media Suppression

**TL;DR:** Military drones struck Radio Tuungane twice in DRC's South Kivu province, with militia threatening journalists over coverage of atrocities. Pattern shows weaponization of surveillance tech against independent media.

## Key Points

- Radio Tuungane in DRC's Minembwe commune hit by drone strikes on March 3 and 26, 2026
- Government-allied Wazalendo militia made calls threatening station over atrocity reporting
- Strikes damaged solar panels, batteries, and broadcasting equipment but staff escaped
- Over 60 journalists and media workers killed by drones globally since 2023 [CPJ]
- Journalists using YouTube-based platforms like Mukirambi TV1 to bypass traditional media constraints
- Multiple reporters in South Kivu forced into hiding due to surveillance and death threats
- National Intelligence Agency (ANR) summoning journalists for 'intelligence purposes'
- Demonstrates escalation from digital censorship to kinetic attacks on media infrastructure

## Deep Dive

## Context and Escalation

The targeting of Radio Tuungane represents a concerning escalation in the weaponization of surveillance technology against independent media. The station operates in rebel-controlled territory using solar power infrastructure, making it a critical information lifeline for communities cut off from government-controlled communications. The precision targeting of solar panels and broadcasting equipment suggests detailed surveillance and intelligence gathering preceding the strikes.

## Technical Infrastructure Under Attack

The attacks reveal how critical technical infrastructure supporting independent media becomes a primary target. Radio Tuungane's reliance on solar power and battery systems mirrors many freedom tech deployments in conflict zones. The militia's specific threats about drone attacks indicate coordination between surveillance capabilities and kinetic operations. Journalists' migration to YouTube-based platforms like Mukirambi TV1 shows adaptation to platform censorship, but physical infrastructure remains vulnerable.

## Surveillance State Tactics

The systematic approach - phone threats in February, drone strikes in March, continued harassment through April - demonstrates sophisticated intimidation campaigns combining digital surveillance with physical threats. The National Intelligence Agency's summoning of journalists for 'intelligence purposes' parallels tactics used globally to map media networks and sources. Multiple journalists going into hiding indicates successful suppression of local information networks.

## Implications for Freedom Tech

This case highlights the critical need for resilient, distributed broadcasting infrastructure that can withstand targeted kinetic attacks. The successful intimidation campaign shows how authoritarian forces are adapting to counter decentralized media strategies, moving from digital censorship to physical destruction of key nodes. Builders should consider how solar-powered broadcasting stations and similar infrastructure can be hardened or made more distributed to resist targeted strikes.
