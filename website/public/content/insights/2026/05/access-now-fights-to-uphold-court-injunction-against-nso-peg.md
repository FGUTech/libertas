# Access Now Fights to Uphold Court Injunction Against NSO Pegasus Targeting WhatsApp

**TL;DR:** Access Now filed amicus brief urging Ninth Circuit to maintain permanent injunction preventing NSO Group from targeting WhatsApp users with Pegasus spyware that bypasses encryption protections.

## Key Points

- Access Now and 10 civil society orgs filed amicus brief supporting WhatsApp vs NSO Group case
- District Court issued permanent injunction forbidding NSO from targeting WhatsApp or users' devices
- Original lawsuit filed after NSO used WhatsApp servers to deliver Pegasus to 1,400+ people across 20 countries
- Pegasus spyware bypasses WhatsApp's encryption to surveil journalists, activists, and human rights defenders
- NSO remains on U.S. Commerce Department Entity List restricting business operations
- Case represents six-year legal battle including failed NSO appeals to Supreme Court
- Brief argues protecting encryption safeguards global digital infrastructure used by billions

## Deep Dive

## Legal Precedent for Encryption Protection

This case represents a critical test of whether U.S. courts will actively protect encrypted communications infrastructure from commercial spyware vendors. The permanent injunction against NSO Group, if upheld, would establish strong legal precedent that companies cannot circumvent encryption protections even for ostensible national security purposes. The District Court's ruling specifically found that NSO's exploitation of WhatsApp's servers constituted unlawful targeting that undermines the security of encrypted messaging platforms.

## Technical and Strategic Implications

Pegasus represents the current state-of-the-art in zero-click exploits that bypass even robust encryption implementations. NSO's business model depends on finding and exploiting vulnerabilities in widely-used platforms like WhatsApp, Signal, and other encrypted messengers. The injunction specifically prevents NSO from targeting WhatsApp's infrastructure, which could force the company to develop alternative attack vectors or limit its operational capabilities against activists and journalists who rely on WhatsApp.

## Broader Freedom Tech Impact

The case occurs within a broader ecosystem where NSO continues operations despite Entity List restrictions, suggesting the company is actively seeking legitimacy and market access. Access Now's brief emphasizes that even under potential U.S. ownership, Pegasus technology would likely continue enabling human rights abuses. The outcome will signal whether courts view encryption protection as a matter of national security and human rights, potentially influencing how other commercial spyware vendors operate.

## What Builders Should Monitor

The Ninth Circuit's decision will indicate whether legal frameworks can effectively constrain commercial surveillance vendors. If upheld, the injunction model could be replicated against other spyware companies targeting encrypted platforms. Builders should watch for technical details that emerge about NSO's attack methodologies and consider how similar legal protections might apply to other freedom tech infrastructure beyond messaging platforms.
