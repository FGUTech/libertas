# China Pressures Zambia to Cancel Major Digital Rights Conference RightsCon

**TL;DR:** Chinese government pressure forced cancellation of RightsCon 2026 in Zambia after officials objected to planned discussions of China's digital authoritarianism and Taiwanese activist participation.

## Key Points

- Access Now cancelled RightsCon conference in Lusaka, Zambia (May 5-8) due to Chinese government pressure
- 2,600+ participants from 100+ countries were expected at premier digital rights gathering
- Chinese officials objected to discussions of China's digital authoritarian practices
- China also demanded exclusion of Taiwanese activists from participation
- Zambian officials backtracked on hosting commitment under diplomatic pressure
- Access Now refused to moderate topics or exclude at-risk communities including Taiwanese participants
- Freedom House categorizes this as malign foreign influence targeting transnational civil society
- Pattern includes similar incidents: Uganda rendered 36 activists from Kenya leadership seminar
- Tanzania deported Kenyan officials monitoring opposition leader's trial in 2025
- Event cancellation disrupts critical coordination on digital rights issues globally

## Deep Dive

## Context and Implications

The cancellation of RightsCon 2026 represents a significant escalation in China's efforts to project its political vision globally and suppress critical discourse about digital authoritarianism. RightsCon is considered the premier gathering for civil society working on human rights and technology, making its cancellation a major blow to the global digital rights community. The Chinese government's ability to pressure Zambia—a sovereign nation—into reversing its hosting commitment demonstrates the reach of Beijing's influence operations.

## Pattern of Transnational Suppression

While Freedom House notes this specific incident doesn't constitute traditional transnational repression (no direct targeting of Chinese dissidents), it represents a sophisticated form of malign foreign influence. The strategy mirrors documented cases across Africa where authoritarian governments coordinate to suppress civil society: Uganda's rendition of 36 activists from a Kenya leadership seminar, and Tanzania's deportation of Kenyan officials monitoring political trials. These actions systematically break down the networks that enable cross-border democratic organizing.

## Strategic Impact on Freedom Tech

The cancellation disrupts critical coordination mechanisms for the global digital rights community at a time when technological authoritarianism is accelerating. RightsCon serves as a key venue for sharing censorship circumvention techniques, coordinating responses to digital repression, and building solidarity networks. China's successful intervention demonstrates how economic leverage can be weaponized to silence discussions of surveillance technologies, internet shutdowns, and other digital rights violations—effectively extending the Great Firewall's censorship beyond China's borders.

## What This Means for Builders

The incident highlights the vulnerability of centralized gathering points for the freedom tech community. When major conferences can be cancelled through state pressure, it underscores the need for more distributed, resilient coordination mechanisms. The targeting of Taiwanese participation also signals escalating pressure on any entities that don't conform to Beijing's political framework, with implications for freedom tech projects that operate across contested geopolitical boundaries.
